## 1. Purpose

This policy protects MedDefense Health Systems' information systems, patient information, and clinical operations from unauthorized access, misuse, disclosure, and security threats.

It establishes mandatory and enforceable rules for using MedDefense technology and handling information to protect patients and support compliance with applicable security and privacy requirements.

## 2. Scope

This policy applies to every person who accesses a MedDefense system, network, account, device, application, or information resource, including:

- Full-time and part-time employees.
- Physicians, nurses, and other clinical employees.
- Administrative and IT employees.
- Contractors and consultants.
- Vendors with remote or on-site access.
- Temporary clinical staff, agency nurses, and visiting medical professionals.

This policy applies at all MedDefense facilities and remote working locations.

It covers MedDefense computers, laptops, clinical workstations, EHR systems, medical imaging systems, patient scheduling systems, email accounts, internal networks, clinical networks, VPN connections, and approved cloud services.

All users must follow this policy regardless of their position, department, or employment arrangement.

## 3. Acceptable Use

MedDefense systems, devices, networks, applications, and accounts exist exclusively for authorized MedDefense business purposes and clinical care delivery.

Users must:

1. Use MedDefense systems only for assigned and authorized responsibilities.
2. Access patient information only when required for an authorized clinical or business purpose.
3. Use individually assigned accounts and approved authentication methods.
4. Use only software, devices, and applications approved by IT Security.
5. Protect MedDefense information from unauthorized access, disclosure, modification, or loss.
6. Lock workstations when unattended.
7. Log out of shared clinical workstations after completing work.
8. Follow approved security and privacy procedures.
9. Immediately report suspected security incidents.
10. Complete required security and privacy training.

Personal use of MedDefense systems is prohibited unless specifically authorized in writing by IT Security.

## 4. Prohibited Activities

The following activities are strictly prohibited.

### 4.1 Unauthorized Patient Record Access

Users must not access, view, search, download, or disclose patient records outside an active care relationship or another specifically authorized job responsibility.

Accessing records out of curiosity, including records belonging to friends, family members, colleagues, or public figures, is prohibited.

### 4.2 Credential Sharing

Users must not:

- Share passwords, PINs, authentication tokens, or MFA codes.
- Allow another person to use their account.
- Use another employee's credentials.
- Approve authentication requests they did not initiate.
- Store passwords in unsecured files or visible locations.

Each user must use only their individually assigned credentials.

### 4.3 Unauthorized Software Installation

Users must not install, download, execute, or configure unauthorized software on any MedDefense endpoint.

This includes unapproved browser extensions, remote access tools, file-sharing applications, and software obtained from untrusted sources.

Required software must be requested through the approved IT process.

### 4.4 Unauthorized Personal Devices

Users must not connect personal laptops, smartphones, tablets, USB storage devices, or other personal equipment to the MedDefense clinical network without prior written IT Security authorization.

Authorization must be obtained before the device is connected.

### 4.5 Unauthorized Data Transmission

Users must not transmit, upload, synchronize, or store MedDefense information using:

- Personal email accounts.
- Consumer cloud storage, including personal Google Drive, Dropbox, or OneDrive accounts.
- Personal messaging applications, including WhatsApp or Telegram.
- Unapproved file-sharing services.
- Unapproved external storage devices.

MedDefense information must be transferred only through approved systems and authorized communication channels.

### 4.6 Unauthorized System Access

Users must not attempt to access systems, databases, network segments, accounts, or information beyond their assigned role.

Users must not:

- Bypass access restrictions.
- Attempt to gain unauthorized administrator privileges.
- Scan internal networks without written authorization.
- Disable security monitoring or endpoint protection.
- Modify security configurations without approval.

Security testing is permitted only when formally authorized by IT Security.

### 4.7 Unauthorized AI and Third-Party Services

Users must not enter, upload, transmit, or process patient information in AI tools, external web services, or third-party platforms without prior IT Security and Privacy approval and an executed, applicable Business Associate Agreement (BAA).

This includes public AI chatbots, transcription services, document analysis tools, and other external applications.

Removing a patient's name does not automatically make information safe to upload.

### 4.8 Security Control Circumvention

Users must not disable, bypass, or interfere with:

- Antivirus or endpoint detection software.
- Firewalls.
- Logging and monitoring systems.
- Multi-factor authentication.
- Access restrictions.
- Encryption protections.
- Approved backup configurations.

Any exception requires prior written IT Security approval.

## 5. Protected Health Information (PHI) Handling

Users must access, use, or disclose only the minimum patient information necessary to perform their authorized duties, except where HIPAA permits or requires otherwise, including access for treatment.

Patient information must remain within approved MedDefense systems and may be shared only with authorized individuals for legitimate clinical, operational, or legally permitted purposes.

## 6. Remote Access

Remote access to MedDefense systems is permitted only when authorized by IT Security and required for approved work activities.

All remote users must:

1. Use an approved MedDefense remote access solution.
2. Authenticate using their assigned account and required MFA.
3. Use a MedDefense-managed device or another device explicitly approved by IT Security.
4. Maintain an active approved VPN connection when accessing MedDefense systems from public or shared Wi-Fi.
5. Prevent unauthorized individuals from viewing patient information or accessing the device.
6. Lock the device whenever unattended.
7. Disconnect remote sessions after completing work.

Accessing MedDefense systems from public or shared Wi-Fi without an active approved VPN connection is strictly prohibited.

Users must not allow family members, friends, or unauthorized individuals to use devices during an active MedDefense session.

IT Security may suspend remote access if these requirements are not met.

## 7. Security Incident Reporting

All users must report suspected or confirmed security incidents immediately upon discovery.

Reportable incidents include:

- Suspicious emails, phishing links, or unexpected attachments.
- Unexpected MFA requests.
- Unusual system behavior, including unknown programs or unexpected file encryption.
- Lost or stolen MedDefense laptops, phones, or storage devices.
- Accidental disclosure of patient information.
- Patient information sent to the wrong recipient.
- Suspected unauthorized access to patient records.
- Suspected account compromise or exposed credentials.
- Unauthorized software or devices connected to MedDefense systems.

### Reporting Procedure

Users must:

1. Immediately contact IT Security by telephone using the official incident contact number in the MedDefense internal directory.
2. Create an IT Security incident ticket through the approved service desk.
3. Provide the discovery time, affected system or device, and a description of the incident.
4. Follow IT Security instructions to preserve evidence and prevent further exposure.

If the ticketing system is unavailable, users must report the incident by telephone immediately and create a ticket when the service becomes available.

Users must not wait until the end of their shift or the next working day.

Users must not conceal incidents, delete evidence, or perform unauthorized investigations.

Accidental mistakes must also be reported.

## 8. Security Monitoring and Enforcement

MedDefense may monitor and review system, account, device, and network activity for legitimate security, compliance, operational, and investigative purposes, subject to applicable law.

Monitoring may include:

- System access logs.
- Account authentication records.
- Network activity.
- Software installation records.
- Patient record access.
- Security alerts and incident records.

Users must cooperate with authorized security investigations.

IT Security may restrict or suspend access when necessary to protect patient information, prevent an active threat, or investigate a suspected violation.

## 9. Consequences of Policy Violations

Violations of this policy may result in corrective or disciplinary action under the MedDefense Human Resources disciplinary process.

Depending on the nature and severity of the violation, consequences may include:

1. Mandatory security retraining.
2. Formal written warning.
3. Temporary suspension of system access.
4. Removal of assigned access privileges.
5. Contract termination for contractors or vendors.
6. Employment disciplinary action, up to and including termination.
7. Referral to regulatory or law enforcement authorities when legally required.

Violations involving patient information may constitute HIPAA violations and, depending on the circumstances and applicable law, may expose individuals to personal civil or criminal liability.

No employee, contractor, manager, or vendor is exempt from this policy.

## 10. Exceptions

Exceptions to this policy require documented approval from IT Security and the appropriate authorized management representative before the activity occurs.

Exception requests must identify:

- Business or clinical justification.
- Affected systems and information.
- Security risks.
- Compensating controls.
- Responsible owner.
- Expiration or review date.

Exceptions involving PHI require Privacy review and must not authorize activities prohibited by law.

Emergency clinical access must follow approved emergency access procedures and be documented and reviewed afterward.

## 11. Security Awareness and Training

All users must complete mandatory security and privacy awareness training before receiving system access and complete required refresher training.

Training must cover:

- Phishing recognition.
- Password and credential protection.
- Suspicious email reporting.
- HIPAA minimum necessary access.
- Lost or stolen device reporting.
- Remote access requirements.
- Appropriate handling of patient information.
- Acceptable use of MedDefense systems.

IT Security and Human Resources must maintain training records showing assigned training, completion dates, outstanding requirements, and corrective actions.

Training assignments must reflect job responsibilities and system access.

Contractors and vendors must provide required training completion evidence before access is granted or renewed.

Failure to complete mandatory training may result in suspension of access until the requirement is satisfied.

### Existing Training Compliance Gap

- Total workforce: 2,000.
- Completed training: 1,460.
- Incomplete training: 540.
- Overall completion rate: 73%.
- Internal completion target: 95%.

IT Security must coordinate make-up training for overdue personnel within 30 days of the approved remediation plan.

IT Security must also:

1. Document role-based training assignments.
2. Establish phishing simulation metrics.
3. Verify contractor training completion.
4. Maintain auditable completion records.
5. Prepare the next training plan for management approval.

These actions address the documented training coverage and evidence gaps.

## 12. Policy Distribution, Acknowledgment, and Review

All new employees must receive, read, and sign this policy during onboarding before system access is granted.

Existing employees must acknowledge the policy annually.

Contractors, vendors with remote access, and temporary clinical staff must acknowledge the policy before access is granted and at least annually while access continues.

Human Resources and IT Security must maintain signed acknowledgment records.

IT Security must distribute the approved policy to all affected personnel and maintain evidence of distribution.

This policy must be reviewed at least annually and whenever significant changes occur in technology, clinical operations, cybersecurity risks, or applicable legal requirements.

Material revisions require CISO approval and communication to affected users.
