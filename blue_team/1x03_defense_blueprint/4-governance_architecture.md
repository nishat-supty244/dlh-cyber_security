### RACI Legend

- **R – Responsible:** Performs the work.
- **A – Accountable:** Ultimately accountable for the outcome and decision.
- **C – Consulted:** Provides input before decisions are made.
- **I – Informed:** Kept informed of progress or outcomes.

# Part 2: Role Definitions

## Data Controller

**Role Holder:** Hospital Executive Management / MedDefense Corporation

The **Data Controller** determines the purposes and means of processing patient data and is ultimately responsible for ensuring compliance with applicable privacy and data protection regulations. MedDefense serves as the Data Controller because it decides why patient information is collected, how it is processed, and how it is managed throughout the organization.

---

## Data Processor

**Role Holder:** MedDefense (as an organization) and approved third-party service providers

The **Data Processor** processes personal data on behalf of the Data Controller according to established instructions. MedDefense performs processing activities during the delivery of clinical services and patient care, while external vendors may also act as processors when handling healthcare data under contractual agreements.

---

## Data Owner

**Role Holder:** Department Heads (e.g., Dr. Patel – Cardiology)

The **Data Owner** is responsible for determining the business value, classification, access permissions, and appropriate use of departmental information. Department Heads fulfill this role because they possess the operational knowledge required to determine who should have access to sensitive clinical records and how those records should be classified.

---

## Data Custodian (Data Steward)

**Role Holder:** IT Director (Sarah) and the IT Team

The **Data Custodian** is responsible for the technical protection and maintenance of organizational data. Responsibilities include implementing security controls, managing storage systems, performing backups, maintaining servers, and ensuring the confidentiality, integrity, and availability of data. Sarah and the IT team fulfill this role by managing the infrastructure that stores and protects organizational information.

# Part 3: The CISO Question

Operating without a permanent Chief Information Security Officer (CISO) exposes MedDefense to several governance and operational risks. Without executive-level cybersecurity leadership, security decisions become fragmented, accountability is weakened, and long-term strategic planning becomes difficult. This lack of centralized leadership also increases the likelihood of inconsistent security practices and creates additional challenges during regulatory compliance assessments and external audits.

Considering MedDefense's limited financial resources and its small five-person IT team, hiring a full-time CISO is unlikely to be cost-effective. A **Virtual Chief Information Security Officer (vCISO)** provides a practical alternative by delivering executive-level cybersecurity leadership, strategic planning, risk management, regulatory compliance guidance, and audit preparation services without the expense of a permanent executive salary.

A vCISO enables MedDefense to strengthen its security governance, improve compliance readiness, and establish a mature cybersecurity strategy while remaining within the organization's budget constraints. This approach offers the greatest balance between effective security leadership and financial sustainability for a mid-sized healthcare organization.
