### RACI Legend

- **Responsible (R):** Performs the work required to complete the task.
- **Accountable (A):** Ultimately accountable for the outcome and final decision.
- **Consulted (C):** Provides input and expertise before decisions are made.
- **Informed (I):** Kept informed about progress and outcomes.

# Part 2: Role Definitions

## Data Controller

**Role Holder:** Hospital Executive Management / MedDefense Corporation

The **Data Controller** determines the purpose and means of processing patient data and holds ultimate legal responsibility for compliance with applicable privacy regulations. MedDefense serves as the Data Controller because it decides why sensitive healthcare data is collected, how it is processed, and how it is managed throughout the organization.

---

## Data Processor

**Role Holder:** MedDefense (as an organization) and any approved third-party vendors

The **Data Processor** performs data processing activities on behalf of the Data Controller according to defined instructions. MedDefense acts as a Data Processor when carrying out clinical workflows and patient care operations, while third-party service providers process data under contractual agreements with MedDefense.

---

## Data Owner

**Role Holder:** Department Heads (e.g., Dr. Patel – Cardiology)

The **Data Owner** is responsible for determining the business classification, usage, and access authorization of specific datasets. Department Heads fulfill this role because they understand the operational importance and sensitivity of their department's information and decide who is authorized to access it.

---

## Data Custodian / Data Steward

**Role Holder:** IT Director (Sarah) and the IT Team

The **Data Custodian (Data Steward)** is responsible for the technical management and protection of organizational data. Responsibilities include maintaining storage systems, performing backups, implementing security controls, and ensuring the confidentiality, integrity, and availability of information. Sarah and the IT team fulfill this role by managing the infrastructure where organizational data is stored and protected.

# Part 3: The CISO Function and Recommendation

Operating without a permanent Chief Information Security Officer (CISO) leaves MedDefense vulnerable to fragmented decision-making, limited executive-level cybersecurity leadership, and reduced accountability during its upcoming external audit. Without a dedicated CISO, security initiatives become reactive and inconsistent, with important decisions relying on ad hoc discussions instead of an organization-wide risk management strategy.

Given the hospital's limited financial resources and its small five-person IT team, MedDefense should outsource the CISO function to a **Virtual Chief Information Security Officer (vCISO)**. Employing a full-time CISO would require a significant executive salary and associated benefits, placing unnecessary strain on the organization's budget. In contrast, a vCISO delivers executive-level cybersecurity leadership, strategic planning, regulatory compliance expertise, risk management oversight, and audit preparation services at a substantially lower cost.

Adopting a vCISO model enables MedDefense to strengthen its security governance, improve regulatory compliance, and establish a mature cybersecurity strategy while remaining financially sustainable. For a mid-sized healthcare organization with limited resources, a vCISO represents the most practical and cost-effective approach to achieving strong cybersecurity leadership.
