# The What-If: Impact Assessment

---

# Scenario A: Experimental Cardiac Clinical Trial

## New Threat Actors

- Nation-state actors
- Corporate competitors

These adversaries are attracted by the value of:

- Proprietary cardiac research data
- Clinical trial results
- Intellectual property

---

## Changed Attack Vectors

- Supply chain compromise becomes highly relevant due to increased access from international research institutions.
- Phishing activity increases, specifically targeting:
  - Lead researchers
  - Clinical trial staff
  - Trial participants

---

## Shifted Priorities

- Supply Chain Compromise moves from **Rank 4 → Rank 2**.
- Protection of:
  - Research integrity
  - Patient privacy
  - Intellectual property

becomes a top-tier security priority.

---

## New Security Gaps

- Increased third-party risk management requirements
- Need for stronger data isolation
- Dedicated server segmentation requirements

---

## Net Assessment

Risk exposure significantly increases due to the introduction of valuable intellectual property that attracts more sophisticated threat actors, including nation-state and competitive intelligence groups.

---

# Scenario B: Cloud-Hosted EHR Migration

## New Threat Actors

- Cloud-native attackers
- Specialized SaaS exploitation researchers

The attack focus shifts from local infrastructure to cloud-based identity and application security.

---

## Changed Attack Vectors

### Reduced Importance

Traditional on-premises attacks become less relevant:

- Flat network exploitation
- Local lateral movement
- Internal infrastructure attacks

### Increased Importance

Cloud-focused attacks become primary concerns:

- API exploitation
- Identity Provider (IdP) compromise
- Misconfigured cloud storage
- Cloud access policy weaknesses

---

## Shifted Priorities

- Local ransomware attacks decrease in priority.
- Identity-based attacks move to **Rank 1**.

The cloud environment becomes primarily protected through identity controls rather than traditional network boundaries.

---

## New Security Gaps

- Misconfigured cloud IAM policies
- Lack of SaaS activity visibility
- Limited cloud security monitoring
- Vendor lock-in risks affecting security controls

---

## Net Assessment

The risk profile shifts from a:

**Physical / Network-Centric Model**

to:

**Identity / Configuration-Centric Model**

With properly implemented cloud security controls, exposure to traditional ransomware risks may decrease. However, identity compromise becomes the primary security concern.

# Scenario C: Public Disclosure of Ransomware History

## New Threat Actors

- Hacktivists
- Opportunistic cybercriminals

These groups are motivated by MedDefense's public reputation as a previously compromised organization.

---

## Changed Attack Vectors

Phishing and social engineering attacks become more significant as attackers exploit:

- Public fear
- Reduced organizational credibility
- Increased employee uncertainty

---

## Shifted Priorities

- Insider Data Theft (**Scenario 2**) moves to **Rank 1**.

Reasons:

- Reduced employee morale
- Increased insider motivation
- Higher likelihood of secondary data theft attempts

---

## New Security Gaps

- Public trust erosion
- Increased regulatory scrutiny
- Potential loss of cybersecurity personnel due to reputational damage

---

## Net Assessment

MedDefense becomes a **"target-rich environment"** where attackers view the organization as a high-probability target for follow-up attacks.

The organization must prepare for:

- Increased phishing attempts
- Reputation-driven attacks
- Insider risk escalation
- Continuous targeting by opportunistic actors
