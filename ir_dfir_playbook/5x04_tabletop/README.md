**Tabletop   – Learning Objectives**

**Technical**

Q1. How do you design scenario injects that test specific organizational capabilities rather than general knowledge?

Answer: Create realistic events (injects) that force participants to demonstrate specific skills, such as incident detection, escalation, communication, containment, and decision-making. Instead of asking what they know, test what they would actually do during an incident.

Q2. Why should a tabletop scenario be mapped to organizational capabilities before writing injects?

Answer: Mapping ensures every inject has a clear purpose and tests a specific capability. For example, a ransomware alert can test detection, while a hospital system outage can test incident escalation and business continuity.

Q3. How do you define objective evaluation criteria for each inject?

Answer: Define measurable expectations before the exercise, such as who must respond, what decision is required, and the expected response time. The facilitator evaluates participants against these criteria rather than personal opinions.

**Conceptual**

Q4. What is the difference between a tabletop exercise and a regular meeting?

Answer: A meeting mainly involves discussion and information sharing. A tabletop exercise uses simulated incidents (injects) to force decisions, test procedures, identify weaknesses, and produce actionable findings.

Q5. Why is facilitator neutrality important during a tabletop exercise?

Answer: A neutral facilitator does not guide participants toward correct answers or protect them from difficult decisions. This helps reveal actual organizational weaknesses rather than producing comfortable but misleading results.

Q6. How can exercise observations be converted into actionable findings?

Answer: Document what happened, what was expected, the gap between them, and its potential impact. Then recommend a corrective action with a responsible owner and deadline.

Example: The team took 25 minutes to escalate a critical incident instead of the required 10 minutes. The finding should identify the escalation gap and assign someone to improve the process.

**Transversal**

Q7. Why should tabletop exercises include stressful and challenging scenarios?

Answer: Real incidents involve uncertainty, time pressure, and competing priorities. Challenging scenarios reveal weaknesses in decision-making, coordination, and procedures. Exercises that are too easy may produce no useful findings.

Q8. Why should exercise artifacts be written so another facilitator can use them independently?

Answer: Complete documentation ensures the exercise can run consistently without its original designer. Each artifact should include objectives, scenario details, inject timing, expected decisions, evaluation criteria, and facilitator instructions.

Q9. Why must exercise findings have owners and deadlines?

Answer: Without an assigned owner and deadline, identified problems may never be fixed. Every finding needs a clear corrective action, a responsible person, and a completion date to ensure accountability.

**Quick Revision – Important Terms**

| **Term**                | **Simple meaning**                                       |
| ----------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------- |
| Tabletop Exercise (TTX) | A discussion-based simulation of a real incident         |
| Inject                  | A new event or information introduced during an exercise |
| Capability              | An organizational ability being tested                   |
| Facilitator             | Person who guides the exercise                           |
| Evaluation Criteria     | Standards used to measure performance                    |
| Decision Point          | Moment when participants must choose an action           |
| Observation             | What actually happened during the exercise               |
| Finding                 | A documented weakness supported by evidence              |
| Corrective Action       | Specific step taken to fix a weakness                    |
| Owner                   | Person responsible for fixing the issue                  |
| Deadline                | Date by which the issue must be resolved                 |

Key concept to remember:

A successful tabletop exercise follows this process:

Capabilities → Scenario → Injects → Decisions → Evaluation → Findings → Corrective Actions → Owners & Deadlines

The goal is not to prove that the organization is prepared. The goal is to discover weaknesses before a real incident happens.
